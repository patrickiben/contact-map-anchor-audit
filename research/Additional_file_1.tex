\section{Additional file 1: Vertex
thinning}\label{additional-file-1-vertex-thinning}

Retained mathematical contribution from the combined manuscript. Vertex
thinning deletes bins and is distinct from sequencing-depth reduction.
The numbering below belongs to this supplement.

\textbf{Proposition 2: finite-set degree-one extinction.} Fix \(N\) bins
and retain each independently with probability \(p\). Restrict the edge
array to the retained bins. Let \(T_p\) be a well-defined statistic that
equals zero whenever the resulting degree-one diagram is empty.
Transformations of the statistic or filtration may depend on \(p\) or
the retained count, provided they preserve this zero. Then, for every
\(\varepsilon > 0\),

\[\Pr(|T_p|>\varepsilon)\leq\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(N,p)\geq4\}\leq \binom{N}{4}p^4.\]

\textbf{Proof.} A flag complex on at most three vertices has no
degree-one homology: any three-cycle is filled by its triangle. A
nonempty degree-one diagram therefore requires at least four retained
vertices. If \(K\) is the retained count, the indicator of \(K \geq  4\)
is bounded by the number of retained four-vertex subsets, whose
expectation is \(\binom{N}{4}p^4\). This proves the bound and
convergence to zero in probability for fixed \(N\).

The result concerns the probability of a nonzero statistic. It does not
give a universal exponent for persistence amplitude, an expectation
bound for arbitrarily rescaled statistics, or a monotonicity law at
finite \(p\). Deleting the hub of a wheel graph can expose a four-cycle
and increase degree-one persistence. Complexes with at most three
vertices need not be contractible; the proof requires only their empty
degree-one diagrams.

If \(N\) grows, \(p\) tending to zero alone is insufficient. Vanishing
expected retained count, \(Np \to  0\), is sufficient for extinction.
Four growing groups arranged around a cycle can retain a degree-one
feature when each group still contains retained vertices. Consequently,
the finite-set argument must not be presented as an impossibility
theorem for ordinary sequencing-depth changes at fixed bin resolution.

Subsampling a retained set to a fixed \(m\) bins is available only when
at least \(m\) remain. For a fixed original bin set and map, under
independent retention with a common probability \(p\), conditional on
eligibility and averaging over the retained set, a subsequent uniform
\(m\)-subset is uniformly distributed among the original \(m\)-subsets.
This yields a specific conditional comparability statement; it does not
recover the full-map target or remove the loss of eligibility. The proof
supplies the finite-sampling boundary directly. It provides no planar
scaling exponent for contact-map summaries.

\textbf{Map-specific refinement.} Let \(a_j\) count \(j\)-bin subsets
whose restricted full filtration has a positive-length H1 interval.
Independent vertex retention gives the exact finite expression

\[\Pr\{D_1(w|R_p)\ne\varnothing\}=\sum_{j=4}^N a_jp^j(1-p)^{N-j}.\]

If \(k\) is the smallest index with \(a_k > 0\), its leading term is
\(a_k p^k + O(p^{k+1})\) for fixed map and \(N\). If all \(a_j\) vanish,
the probability is zero. Thus the universal fourth-power upper bound is
sharp in order only when the map contains a four-bin witness. A
five-cycle with perimeter births zero and all chord births one has
probability exactly \(p^5\). Additional retained vertices may fill a
witness, so the event is not monotone. This refinement concerns diagram
nonemptiness; a particular statistic can still vanish on a nonempty
diagram. Exhaustive four- and five-cycle checks accompany the proof.
